How these ownership projections are measured

FTA's ownership models are machine-learning models that retrain and re-predict around the clock as news, lineups, and salaries change, so the projections keep getting sharper as a slate approaches. The NBA model's average error is 5.8 ownership points across 9,981 graded player projections.

NBA ownership can flip in the hour before tip-off. A late scratch opens up minutes for a teammate, and by the time starting lineups are confirmed, a player who sat at 4% that morning can be pushing 20%.

Minutes drive more of the movement here than in any other sport, since one starter's absence can hand a bench piece a full role overnight. On single-game showdown slates, watch the gap between a player's flex ownership and his Captain/MVP ownership too — those two numbers tell different stories about how the field is building around him.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are NBA DFS ownership projections?

Ownership projections estimate the percentage of DFS lineups that will roster a specific player in a given contest. High-ownership players (25%+) are popular picks, while low-ownership players (under 10%) offer leverage in tournaments if they hit their ceiling.

How do ownership projections help in DFS?

In tournaments (GPPs), rostering low-ownership players who outperform creates separation from the field. If a 5%-owned player has a huge game, lineups with that player gain a massive edge. Ownership projections help you find contrarian plays without sacrificing projection quality.

How are NBA ownership projections calculated?

FTA's ownership models retrain and re-predict around the clock as news, lineups, and salaries change, so the projections keep getting sharper as a slate approaches. Players in high-pace favorable matchups at affordable salaries typically project for the highest ownership.
